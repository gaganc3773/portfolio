Residual Learnability Score (RLS)

A calibrated, interpretable test for a question a loss value can't answer: has a model captured all the learnable signal — or is it still leaving structure on the table?

Validation loss tells you how wrong a model is, not whether it is as right as it can be. Two models with identical held-out error can be in completely different situations — one has extracted all the learnable signal and leaves only irreducible noise; the other is systematically missing a periodicity, an interaction, or a region of input space. An adequate model leaves residuals that behave like white noise; a deficient one leaves residuals that are still predictable. RLS makes that concrete: it measures the fraction of the residual field's variance a detector can recover out-of-sample — a detector-relative quantity that ties the score directly to the theory of usable (V-)information.

01 · VERDICT
Calibrated p-value
A permutation null (shuffled residuals) rebuilds the "no-structure" distribution. A detector that hallucinates structure hallucinates equally on shuffles, so its overfitting bias cancels — the test stays calibrated even with an arbitrarily powerful detector.
02 · FINGERPRINT
What was missed
A Fourier Neural Operator detector fits residuals in the frequency domain, so the statistic doubles as a description of the missed structure — its scale, orientation, and complexity (or, for tabular models, the responsible features).
03 · RECOVERABILITY
Proven effect size
Build the correction the fingerprint implies and measure the held-out error drop. Theory predicts it equals the RLS fraction; when out-of-sample reduction matches the in-sample score, the missed structure is proven real.

One-sided by design. A significant score certifies a model is improvable; a null result certifies nothing — it may mean the model is adequate, or that the detector was too weak. Reporting the detector alongside the score is non-negotiable, and cross-fitting makes memorized noise score zero.

Validated through six pre-registered studies: near-perfect noise/signal separation (ROC-AUC 0.998) at the nominal false-positive rate; in-sample score predicting held-out error reduction almost one-to-one (R² 0.97–0.99, including 1-D Burgers PDE residual fields with the FNO detector on GPU); exact recovery of injected scale / orientation / complexity; and a detector sweep confirming every class up to a full FNO reads zero on pure noise. On real models it flagged a linear regressor — fingerprint pointing at the geographic and nonlinear features it structurally can't express, ~45% of held-out error recovered — while clearing a strong gradient-boosting model. Packaged as an open-source Python library.

Why this is interpretability work — and where it goes next

RLS is, at its core, a probing method with the honesty machinery interpretability has been missing. The field's central worry about probes — that a probe "finding" a feature may be reading its own capacity rather than the model's computation — is exactly what the permutation null, out-of-sample estimation, and recoverability check are built to neutralize, grounding the apparatus in usable information rather than raw accuracy. The same procedure transfers directly to language models: applied to an LLM's next-token residuals or its residual-stream activations, it asks whether a candidate direction carries real, causally-recoverable signal or is an artifact — and localizes what the model has failed to represent — a natural bridge to the mechanistic interpretability of large language models.

Operator Learning for Forward & Inverse PDEs

Research Intern · Prof. Shashi Jain, IISc Bangalore · Jan 2026 – Present
🔒 Ongoing · details on request

Building Fourier Neural Operator / PINO surrogates that learn family-wide solution maps for parametric PDEs in place of per-instance classical solvers. Reproduced standard operator-learning benchmarks — Burgers (12.9% relative L2 with a PDE loss vs. 23.4% for a data-only FNO) and Darcy flow (1.3% relative L2). The work extends to forward operator surrogates, inverse coefficient recovery, and calibration problems whose methods and quantitative results are part of ongoing unpublished research.

Neural Quantum State Tomography (PINN-QST)

Physics-informed reconstruction · single forward pass
🔒 Unpublished · details on request

A physics-informed network that reconstructs a quantum state's density matrix in a single forward pass and is physically valid by construction — where classical linear inversion becomes mostly unphysical as the system grows. In the data-scarce, low-measurement regime it is competitive with, and at small system sizes beats, per-sample maximum-likelihood estimation, while running orders of magnitude faster; it also stays high-fidelity on hard pure and entangled states (e.g. GHZ). A purpose-built physics-aware training design concentrates the gains exactly where reconstruction is hardest. Methods and full results are unpublished.
