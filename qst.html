Neural Quantum State Tomography

Reconstructing a quantum state's density matrix in a single forward pass — physically valid by construction, and competitive with maximum-likelihood estimation in the low-data regime.

Quantum State TomographyPINNs Density MatricesPhysical Validity Amortized Inference

Quantum state tomography infers a state's density matrix from measurement statistics. The classical options each have a weakness: linear inversion is fast but returns increasingly unphysical estimates as the system grows, and maximum-likelihood estimation is accurate but re-runs a per-state optimization every time. A physics-informed network instead emits a guaranteed-physical state in a single forward pass, amortized across many states.

  • Physically valid at every system size, where linear inversion becomes mostly unphysical as dimension grows.
  • Competitive with — and at small system sizes better than — per-sample MLE in the data-scarce, low-measurement regime, while running orders of magnitude faster.
  • Stays high-fidelity on hard pure and entangled states (e.g. GHZ), the regime where a learned prior helps most.
Entangled-state results are simulated (no quantum hardware). Exact figures, the training design, and full comparisons are unpublished — available on request.

The reconstruction is parameterized so that the output is a manifestly physical density matrix by construction, and the network is trained in an amortized way across states and measurement budgets. A purpose-built, physics-aware objective concentrates accuracy exactly where reconstruction is hardest.

The specifics of the objective and architecture are unpublished.
