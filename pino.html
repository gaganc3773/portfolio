Operator Learning for Forward & Inverse PDEs

Learning family-wide solution maps for parametric PDEs — one trained operator in place of a per-instance classical solver — and inverting them to recover hidden coefficients.

FNO / PINOPyTorch Inverse ProblemsCrank–Nicolson Adam → L-BFGS

A classical PDE solver runs from scratch for every new coefficient, boundary condition, or payoff. A neural operator instead learns a mapping between function spaces, so a single trained model returns the solution for an entire family in one forward pass. That reframing opens up three problems at once: fast forward surrogates, inverse recovery of latent coefficients from observations, and calibration of models to data.

Reproduced standard operator-learning benchmarks as a baseline — Burgers (≈12.9% relative L2 with a PDE-informed loss vs. ≈23.4% for a data-only FNO) and Darcy flow (≈1.3% relative L2).

The work extends to a universal forward pricer spanning multiple payoff types, an inverse local-volatility calibration that bypasses iterative classical schemes, and recovery of latent PDE coefficients to high accuracy by differentiating through frozen operators with an Adam → L-BFGS schedule.

Novel methods and quantitative results beyond the public benchmarks are part of ongoing unpublished research — available on request.

Fourier Neural Operators parameterize the map through learnable weights in the spectral domain, giving resolution-independent surrogates. Forward training uses PDE-informed objectives; inverse problems are solved by descending through a frozen forward operator to recover the coefficient that best explains the observations.

Details of the inverse and calibration methods are unpublished.
